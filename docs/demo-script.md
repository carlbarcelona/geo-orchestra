# Demonstration script (about 4–5 minutes)

Start from a fresh browser, at full screen and in the foreground.

1. **Problem (20 s).** EIC visualizations are visual only. GeoOrchestra makes NASA rainfall audible *and* inspectable.
2. **Preset mode → Start listening** (one click). Point at the caption and the guide: "the hum is a normal month; above it is wetter, below drier; a bell is an unusual month." Then switch to **Advanced** at the top right to show that every control is still there.
3. **Numerical → NASA sample.** "295 months of IMERG V07B over the Philippines, prepared from NASA's archive and validated (295/295 months, 5/5 spot checks)."
4. **Select a region.** Choose Mindanao in Pins / Regions, then open Map and click a cell near Manila: "a single cell versus an area mean, labelled differently."
5. **Musical sonification.** Press Space (the first click enables audio). "Higher pitch means more rain; above the hum means wetter than normal." Point at the shared cursor moving across the graph, table and map.
6. **Geophony.** Switch mode: the same measurements become rain density.
7. **Inspect.** Pause, click a note, open Event details: date, area mean, original mm/hr, note, the normal and how the month compares, mapping rain-v2.
8. **Honesty.** Load the Synthetic demo to show a missing month (a red gap with no note). Then load the NASA sample again.
9. **Visual feed.**
   1. Import the authentic SVS 5478 frames.
   2. Show that audio stays disabled until measurements are connected.
   3. Connect the climatology and play. Press **S**: "11 beats west to east; pitch is latitude, so you hear the rain band sit higher in July."
   4. Explain: "seasonal average, not a historical event; the linkage is a reconstruction; the frames include Feb 29, which NASA's own date list omits. We read every frame's date from its overlay."
10. **Close.** Audio legend, keyboard control and the accessibility options.

Fallback: a recording or screenshots, clearly labelled as such.
